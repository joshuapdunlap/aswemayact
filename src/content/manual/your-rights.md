The Arkansas Freedom of Information Act, Ark. Code Ann. § 25-19-101 and the sections that follow it, lets you see and copy the records your state and local governments keep. You do not need to be a reporter or a lawyer, and in general you do not need to say why you want them.

<p class="law">AG Handbook, Questions &amp; Answers: “A requester’s purpose or motive in seeking access to particular records is irrelevant.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 35 -->

## Records in any form

A public record is not only paper. The law reaches email and other electronic files, databases, photographs, audio and video, whatever the medium.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-103(15)(A): “writings, recorded sounds, films, tapes, electronic or computer-based information, or data compilations in any medium”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 7 -->

Anything kept in a public office, or by a public employee doing the job, starts out presumed to be public. It stays open unless a specific exemption applies; [Exemptions that matter](/manual/exemptions/) covers the ones you are most likely to meet.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-103(15)(A): “All records maintained in public offices or by public employees within the scope of their employment shall be presumed to be public records”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 7 -->

What counts is what a record is about, not where it is stored. A personal note on a work computer is not public because of the computer; a message about public business can be.

<p class="law">Pulaski Cnty. v. Ark. Democrat-Gazette (2007), as the handbook summarizes it: “Whether a document is a ‘public record’ ultimately depends on its content.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 28 -->

The law covers state agencies, cities, counties, school districts and other bodies that run on public money.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-103(15)(A): “wholly or partially supported by public funds or expending public funds”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 7 -->

## Using the right

<ol class="step-list" role="list">
<li>

Find the custodian: the person in charge of the record, usually a clerk, a records officer or a department's FOIA contact.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-103(2)(A): “Custodian” means “the person having administrative control of that record.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 5 -->

</li>
<li>

Ask by whatever means the office accepts: in person, by phone, by mail, by fax, by email or through an online form. A written request is the one that leaves a trail, and it is the one that triggers the written answer described below.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(2)(B): a request may be made “in person, by telephone, by mail, by facsimile transmission, by electronic mail, or by other electronic means provided by the custodian.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 8 -->

</li>
<li>

Look at the records or take copies during the office's ordinary hours. You may copy them yourself with a phone camera or a scanner.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(1)(A): “open to inspection and copying … by any citizen of the State of Arkansas during the regular business hours of the custodian of the records.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 8 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(1)(A): “copying through image capture, including still and moving photography and video and digital recording”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 8 -->

</li>
<li>

Expect the records now, not in three days. The exception is narrow and has to be put in writing; see [The clock](/manual/the-clock/). What copies may cost is on [What it costs](/manual/what-it-costs/).

</li>
</ol>

## A written answer

Since [Act 879 of 2023](https://arkleg.state.ar.us/Acts/FTPDocument?path=%2FACTS%2F2023R%2FPublic%2F&file=879.pdf&ddBienniumSession=2023%2F2023R), a custodian who receives a written request must answer in writing, within the time the law allows, in three situations.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(3): “A custodian shall respond as follows in writing within the time period required under this section to a written request for public records”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 8 -->

If no responsive records exist, the answer must say so. The wording of that duty, and how to ask for a plain statement, are on [Writing a request](/manual/write-a-request/).

If records exist but the custodian says they are exempt, the answer must name the exemption. "Confidential" or "not subject to FOIA" with no citation is not that answer.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(3)(B): “If any responsive records that exist are subject to exemptions under this chapter or other law, the custodian shall respond and identify the applicable exemptions”</p>
<!-- handbook pp. 8–9 -->

If the office you asked does not control the records, it must tell you who does, when it knows or can easily find out.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(3)(C): “the custodian shall respond and identify the appropriate custodian to direct that request to, if known or readily ascertainable.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 9 -->

## What the law does not do

It does not make every record public: exemptions remove some records or parts of records. It does not open records to everyone: the right belongs to Arkansas citizens, which [Who may request](/manual/who-may-request/) explains. And it does not give you a referee. Arkansas has no FOIA ombudsman and no commission that enforces the act, as the [Reporters Committee's Arkansas guide](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/arkansas/) puts it; when a custodian will not budge, the routes are the ones on [Denied, now what](/manual/denied/).
