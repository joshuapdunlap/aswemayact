In Arkansas the rule is simple: a copy may cost what the copy costs, and nothing for the time people spend finding, reading or redacting it. Two later sections of the law make exceptions, and you should know where they are.

## Copies at cost

The fee may cover the paper, disc or drive the copy is made on, supplies, and the equipment and its upkeep.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(d)(3)(A)(i): “any fee for copies shall not exceed the actual costs of reproduction, including the costs of the medium of reproduction, supplies, equipment, and maintenance”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 15 -->

It does not include the pay of the people who handle your request.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(d)(3)(A)(i): “but not including existing agency personnel time associated with searching for, retrieving, reviewing, or copying the records.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 15 -->

The custodian may add what it actually costs to mail the copies or send them electronically.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(d)(3)(A)(ii): “the actual costs of mailing or transmitting the record by facsimile or other electronic means.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 15 -->

If a record mixes open and exempt information, separating the two is the office's expense, not yours.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(f)(4): “the custodian shall bear the cost of the separation.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 15 -->

## Looking is free

You pay nothing to inspect a record, and nothing to copy it yourself with a phone or camera.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(d)(3)(A)(v): “the custodian may not charge a fee for the requestor’s inspection or copying, including without limitation copying through image capture”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 15 -->

## The $25 line

A custodian may require payment before making copies only when the estimated fee is more than $25. Below that, the copies come first.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(d)(3)(A)(iii): “If the estimated fee exceeds twenty-five dollars ($25.00), the custodian may require the requester to pay that fee in advance.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 15 -->

## Ask what you are paying for

Every charge has to be broken down for you.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(d)(3)(B): “The custodian shall provide an itemized breakdown of charges”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 15 -->

The custodian may also lower or waive the fee when the request is mainly noncommercial and serves the public. That is the custodian's call, not a right, but it costs nothing to ask.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(d)(3)(A)(iv): “requested primarily for noncommercial purposes and that waiver or reduction of the fee is in the public interest.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 15 -->

## Two exceptions

**Custom data work.** A custodian does not have to build you a new report from a database (see [Writing a request](/manual/write-a-request/)), but may choose to.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-109(a)(1): “At his or her discretion, a custodian may agree to summarize, compile, or tailor electronic data”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 23 -->

If the custodian agrees, staff time on that work beyond two hours can be charged.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-109(b)(1): “may charge the actual, verifiable costs of personnel time exceeding two (2) hours associated with the tasks”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 24 -->

**Police, jail and dispatch recordings.** Law enforcement agencies, dispatch centers, jails and their record vendors have their own fee rule for audio and video. A request that takes three hours or less of staff and equipment time is free apart from the media. Past three hours, the charge can be no more than $20 for each hour of the recording's running time, prorated, and the agency may ask for payment in advance.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-112(c)(2)(A): “Requires three (3) hours or less of personnel and equipment time to fulfill the request shall be provided at no charge”</p>
<!-- handbook pp. 25–26 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-112(c)(2)(B): “shall be charged at a rate that does not exceed twenty dollars ($20.00) per hour on a prorated basis for each hour of running time”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 26 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-112(c)(2)(C): a request estimated to take more than three hours “may be required to be prepaid.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 26 -->

The free first three hours are lost if you, or the organization you ask for, made a request under that section in the previous thirty days.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-112(c)(2)(A)(ii): “Unless the requestor or the requestor’s entity has made a request under this section in the immediately preceding thirty-day period”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 26 -->

## Keeping the bill down

<ol class="step-list" role="list">
<li>

Ask for an itemized estimate before any copies are made. If the estimate names staff time for an ordinary request, point to subsection (d)(3)(A)(i) above and ask for a corrected one.

</li>
<li>

Ask for electronic copies sent by email or download, which spares paper and postage. How to ask for a format is on [Writing a request](/manual/write-a-request/).

</li>
<li>

Go and look first. Inspection is free, and you can photograph what you need.

</li>
<li>

If your request is for public, noncommercial use, say so and ask for a waiver.

</li>
<li>

For recordings, narrow the request by date, time and camera so it stays under three hours of work.

</li>
</ol>

Other statutes set their own fees for a few kinds of records, such as certain driver records ([RCFP guide](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/arkansas/)). If a fee is quoted under another law, ask which one.
