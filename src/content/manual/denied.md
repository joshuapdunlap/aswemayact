There is no ombudsman or commission to appeal to in Arkansas ([RCFP guide](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/arkansas/)). That leaves two kinds of moves: the ones you make with the office, and a lawsuit.

## Before court

<ol class="step-list" role="list">
<li>

**Read the answer closely.** Did it say in writing whether records exist? Did it name an exemption? An answer that does neither falls short of the written-response duty on [Your rights](/manual/your-rights/#a-written-answer). Reply and ask for the statement the law requires. A knowing failure to give it carries the penalty at the end of this page.

</li>
<li>

**Narrow and refile.** If the answer was "too broad" or "we couldn't find it," send a narrower request that names document types, dates and people ([Writing a request](/manual/write-a-request/)). If the answer was that the record holds exempt information, ask for it with the exempt parts blacked out. The office may not refuse the whole record for that reason.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(f)(1): “No request … shall be denied on the ground that information exempt from disclosure is commingled with nonexempt information.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 15 -->

</li>
<li>

**Ask a different office for the same record.** An exemption that protects one office's papers may not protect another office's copy. The clearest case is the working-papers exemption, which [Exemptions that matter](/manual/exemptions/#working-papers-follow-the-office) explains. Ask the city, county, school district or agency that received or sent the message.

</li>
<li>

**For personnel or evaluation records, ask the Attorney General.** You may ask for an opinion on the custodian's decision. The window is short: before the Attorney General's office closes on the next business day. The opinion is due within three working days; [Exemptions that matter](/manual/exemptions/) has the rest.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(c)(3)(B)(i): “Either the custodian, requester, or the subject of the records may, before the close of the Attorney General’s office the following business day”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 14 -->

</li>
</ol>

## Going to court

Any citizen who is denied may go straight to circuit court. A case against a state agency goes to Pulaski County Circuit Court or the circuit court where you live; a case against a city, county, school district or other local body goes to the circuit court for that place.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-107(a): “Any citizen denied the rights granted to him or her by this chapter may appeal immediately from the denial”</p>
<!-- handbook pp. 21–22 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-107(a): “to the Pulaski County Circuit Court or to the circuit court of the residence of the aggrieved party, if the State of Arkansas … is involved”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 22 -->

On written application, the court must set a hearing within seven days.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-107(b): “to fix and assess a day the petition is to be heard within seven (7) days of the date of the application of the petitioner”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 22 -->

If you get a significant part of what you asked for after filing, the court must make the defendant pay your reasonable attorney's fees and costs, unless the defendant's position was "substantially justified."

<p class="law">§ 25-19-107(d)(1): “the court shall assess against the defendant reasonable attorney’s fees … unless the court finds that the position of the defendant was substantially justified.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 22 -->

Against the State itself the rule is different. The court may not award fees against a state department, agency or institution; a plaintiff who substantially prevailed files a claim with the Arkansas State Claims Commission instead, within sixty days of the final disposition.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-107(e)(1): “the court shall not assess reasonable attorney’s fees or other litigation expenses reasonably incurred by a plaintiff against the State of Arkansas”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 22 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-107(e)(2)(B): “within sixty (60) days of the final disposition of the appeal”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 22 -->

## The deadline that ends cases

A lawsuit has to be formally served on the defendant. Rule 4(i) of the Arkansas Rules of Civil Procedure allows 120 days from filing. The court can extend that only on a motion made before the time runs out and a showing of good cause; otherwise the case is dismissed. In *Campbell v. Sanders*, a Pulaski County case against the Governor's office, the suit was dismissed on May 7, 2024, after the Attorney General's office argued that the summons and complaint were not delivered within 120 days of filing ([Arkansas Advocate, May 7, 2024](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2024/05/07/judge-dismisses-foia-lawsuit-against-arkansas-governor-by-blogger-who-revealed-lectern-purchase/); [Attorney General's release](https://arkansasag.gov/news-release/pulaski-county-circuit-court-grants-attorney-generals-motion-dismisses-bloggers-lawsuit-against-governor/)). A fast hearing does not help a case that is never served.

## Help with the cost

The [Knight FOI Fund](https://www.nfoic.org/knight/knight-foi-fund-faqs), run by the National Freedom of Information Coalition, accepts applications from "any citizen needing support for a meritorious open government case." It pays up-front litigation costs such as court costs, filing fees and depositions, and only rarely attorney fees. It asks whether you have a lawyer.

## Finding a lawyer

Two places to start:

- The Arkansas Press Association, (501) 374-1500: the number the Attorney General's [2026 handbook](https://media.ark.org/ag/APA-045-2026-FOIA-Handbook-22nd-Edition.pdf) gives under Sources of FOIA Assistance, and the one on the association's [contact page](https://arkansaspress.org/contact-us/). A [national directory](https://www.nfoic.org/foi-hotlines/) also lists an APA FOI hotline at (501) 372-0800. It is a press association's line; ask whether it can refer you.
- The [Reporters Committee's Open Government Guide for Arkansas](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/arkansas/), written by Arkansas lawyers and law professors. It is a detailed free account of how Arkansas courts read the act, and its authors are named.

## A criminal penalty

A negligent violation of the act is a misdemeanor.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-104: “Any person who negligently violates any of the provisions of this chapter shall be guilty of a Class C misdemeanor.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 8 -->

The same penalty reaches a custodian who knowingly fails to give the written answer a written request requires.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(5): “If a custodian knowingly fails to respond as required under subdivision (a)(3) of this section, he or she shall be subject to the penalties in § 25-19-104”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 9 -->
