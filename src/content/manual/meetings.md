The records half of the act lets you see what a public body wrote down. The meetings half lets you watch it decide. [Act 505 of 2025](https://arkleg.state.ar.us/Acts/FTPDocument?path=%2FACTS%2F2025R%2FPublic%2F&file=505.pdf&ddBienniumSession=2025%2F2025R) rewrote that half, and this page follows the section as it now reads.

## Which meetings

The rules reach the formal meetings of a governing body: a city council, a quorum court, a school board, a state board or commission, or any other body that runs on public money.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-103(14)(A): “Public meeting” means “the formal gathering together, in a special or regular gathering, of a governing body, whether in person or remotely.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 7 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-103(13): “Public entity” includes “All other boards, bureaus, commissions, or organizations in the State of Arkansas, except grand juries, supported wholly or in part by public funds or expending public funds”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 7 -->

The section opens with five conditions a meeting has to meet to be lawful: notice, executive sessions held by the rules, a recording, reasonable access for the public, and deliberation the public can hear.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(a): “To lawfully hold a public meeting, a governing body shall ensure that”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 16 -->

## Notice and the agenda

You are entitled to know when and where a body meets, and to see its most current agenda when you ask for it. The deadlines, including the online posting rules, are on [The clock](/manual/the-clock/#meetings-notice-and-agenda).

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(b)(3)(B)(i): “The governing body shall furnish the most current agenda for a public meeting upon request”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 17 -->

## Recorded, and kept for a year

Every public meeting has to be recorded so that the sound is captured, whether as audio, as video or as a broadcast that can be recorded.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(d)(1): “a public meeting shall be recorded in a manner that allows for the capture of sound”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 18 -->

The body has to keep the recording for at least a year, in a form it can copy for you.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(d)(3): “shall be maintained by a public entity for a minimum of one (1) year from the date of the public meeting.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 18 -->

Two things are left out: executive sessions, and volunteer fire departments.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(d)(5): the recording rule “does not apply to: (A) Executive sessions; or (B) Volunteer fire departments.”</p>
<!-- handbook pp. 18–19 -->

A recording is a public record like any other. Ask for it within the year, and ask for the original audio or video file. The [trail on meetings](/trails/the-meeting/) follows two cases in which missing or silent recordings were the complaint.

## Executive sessions

A body may close part of a meeting only for a short list of reasons. The one you will meet most often is a personnel matter.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(c)(1)(A)(i): “Considering the employment, appointment, promotion, demotion, disciplining, or resignation of any public officer or employee.”</p>
<!-- handbook pp. 17–18 -->

Before going in, the body has to say which of those purposes it is meeting for.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(c)(1)(A)(ii): “a governing body shall state publicly which specific purpose listed in subdivision (c)(1)(A)(i) of this section is the basis for the executive session.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 18 -->

The other reasons are narrow: responding to an attack on the body's cybersecurity, a state licensing board preparing its examinations, and the security of a public water system or a city-owned utility. A closed session may not be used to get around the act.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(c)(2): “Executive sessions shall not be called for the purpose of defeating the reason or the spirit of this chapter.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 18 -->

Nothing is decided behind the door. A body that wants to act on what it discussed has to vote in public when the session ends.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(i)(3): “An action taken in an executive session is void unless the governing body conducts a public vote on the matter discussed in the executive session at the conclusion of the executive session.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 21 -->

## Attending, in person or remotely

The body has to give Arkansas residents reasonable access to its meetings, including remote access when it uses remote means.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(e)(1): “A governing body shall ensure that Arkansas residents have reasonable access to attend a public meeting, including through remote means, if such remote means are utilized by the governing body.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 19 -->

Members generally have to be in the room to count toward a quorum or to vote. Cities, counties and school districts may not adopt a policy letting members attend remotely; school districts follow their own statute, and any body may meet remotely during a disaster emergency the Governor declares. When even one member attends remotely, the public may attend the same way, and the notice must say how.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(e)(8)(A): “The governing body shall enable members of the public to attend the public meeting by the same means that the members of the governing body attending the public meeting remotely are attending the public meeting”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 19 -->

## No meeting outside the meeting

The decisions belong in the room. A member may not take part in a poll: a series of messages between staff or agents and members, to find out how members will vote or where they stand.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(f)(1)(A): a member shall not participate in a communication that “He or she knows or reasonably should know is a poll.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 20 -->

Nor may members talk among themselves, outside a meeting, about a matter the body will foreseeably act on. Sharing background facts is allowed. Deliberation is not.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(f)(2)(A): a communication that “Occurs outside of a public meeting with another member of the governing body about a matter on which official action will foreseeably be taken by the governing body.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 20 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(f)(2)(C): “deliberation may only occur at a public meeting of the governing body.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 20 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(g): “An informal meeting that includes deliberation or that is for the purpose of exercising a responsibility, authority, power, or duty of a governing body is strictly prohibited.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 20 -->

Staff may still talk to one another, and to members, about the work, so long as they do not carry votes or positions between members.

## What a court can undo

A court that finds a violation may throw out what the body did at the unlawful meeting, and may throw out action that came out of a prohibited poll or informal meeting.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-106(i)(1): “the circuit court may invalidate any action the governing body took at the unlawful public meeting.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 21 -->

Votes authorizing bonds and other debt run on a thirty-day clock, set out on [The clock](/manual/the-clock/#bond-votes-thirty-days). The suit itself, the seven-day hearing and fee shifting are on [Denied, now what](/manual/denied/#going-to-court), and the criminal penalty there covers the meetings rules too.

## At the next meeting

<ol class="step-list" role="list">
<li>

Ask the body, in writing, to tell you the time and place of its regular meetings and to send its agenda. Keep a copy of the request.

</li>
<li>

If the body goes into executive session, note the purpose it states and the time. When it comes back, watch for the public vote on what it discussed.

</li>
<li>

If members attend by phone or video, check that the notice told the public how to attend the same way.

</li>
<li>

Within the year, [write a request](/manual/write/) for the recording and the minutes, and ask for the original file. Compare them with the agenda.

</li>
<li>

If something went wrong, write down the date, the agenda item and what happened, while it is fresh. The routes from there are on [Denied, now what](/manual/denied/).

</li>
</ol>
