A request is a short letter, and every sentence in it can matter later. Several recent Arkansas cases turned on what a request did or did not say. The steps below come from those cases. The [letter builder](/manual/write/) puts them into a letter you can send.

## Eight things to put in writing

<ol class="step-list" role="list">
<li>

**Address the custodian and say you are an Arkansas citizen.** One sentence covers both; see [Who may request](/manual/who-may-request/).

</li>
<li>

**Be specific enough to find.** Give dates, names, offices, subjects and, where you know them, file or contract numbers.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(2)(C): “The request shall be sufficiently specific to enable the custodian to locate the records with reasonable effort.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 8 -->

</li>
<li>

**Name the kinds of documents.** List them: contracts, non-disclosure agreements, invoices, purchase orders, calendars, emails, text messages, meeting recordings, logs. In Fayetteville in 2026, a resident asked for records about a drone manufacturer's facility. The city released emails about plans to sign a non-disclosure agreement, but not the agreement. Washington County Circuit Judge Doug Martin ruled for the city, writing, "Because Plaintiff did not request non-disclosure agreements" ([Arkansas Advocate, June 18, 2026](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2026/06/18/arkansas-judge-sides-with-city-in-open-records-lawsuit-over-drone-manufacturer/)). A request for "communications" may not reach a contract.

</li>
<li>

**Ask for the format.** Ask for the records in the readily available electronic format, or in a format they can readily be converted to with the office's existing software. Spreadsheets as spreadsheets; recordings as files.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(d)(2)(B): “in any medium in which the record is readily available or in any format to which it is readily convertible with the custodian’s existing software”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 14 -->

The office does not have to create a record that does not exist, so ask for the records it has, not for answers to questions.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(d)(2)(C): “A custodian is not required to compile information or create a record in response to a request made under this section.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 14 -->

</li>
<li>

**For recordings, ask for the original files and the devices.** In 2024 Jimmie Cavin asked the State Police for the make, model and identification number of the devices used to record interviews, and the original media file each device created. His lawsuit alleged that the interview audio he received came in a format typically used for video, which it said “suggests that the videos were intentionally removed” ([Arkansas Advocate via The Arkadelphian, Aug. 7, 2024](https://arkadelphian.com/2024/08/07/arkansas-advocate-lawsuit-alleges-state-police-violated-public-records-law/)). An original file and a device list are harder to answer with an edited copy. Fees for police recordings follow their own rule; see [What it costs](/manual/what-it-costs/).

</li>
<li>

**Ask for a plain statement of what exists.** A written request must get a written answer. If nothing responsive exists, the answer must say so; if records exist but are withheld, it must name the exemption ([Your rights](/manual/your-rights/#a-written-answer)). Ask for that statement for each item you list.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(3)(A): “If no records exist that are responsive to the request, the custodian shall respond that no records exist”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 8 prints "exists"; Act 879 of 2023, § 1, reads "exist" -->

In Saline County in 2026, the county attorney answered one item with "To my understanding, there are no records responsive to request #3." Cavin sued, arguing that § 25-19-105(a)(3) calls for a clear statement that records exist, are exempt or do not exist, not "conditional or equivocal language" ([Benton Courier](https://www.bentoncourier.com/news/cavin-sues-county-officials-over-foia-responses/article_63bdb15d-1600-43fa-87eb-e802c476168c.html)). Number your items so each one can be answered.

</li>
<li>

**Ask for an itemized estimate before copies are made.** The rules are on [What it costs](/manual/what-it-costs/).

</li>
<li>

**Keep a dated copy.** Send by email when you can, and keep the sent message. The dates matter for [The clock](/manual/the-clock/).

</li>
</ol>

## If part of it is exempt

Ask that any exempt material be redacted and the rest released, with each redaction marked. [Exemptions that matter](/manual/exemptions/#exempt-parts-not-exempt-records) quotes the rule.

## Before you send it

Read it as the custodian will. Could an honest clerk find every item from what you wrote? Could a reluctant one answer the letter while leaving out the record you most want? If so, name that record by type, date and parties.
