An exemption is a specific provision that takes a record, or part of one, out of the act. The act lists its own in § 25-19-105(b) and (c), and other Arkansas statutes add more. Courts read them narrowly, and a record that does not fit squarely inside one is open.

<p class="law">AG Handbook, Questions &amp; Answers: “If a public record does not fall squarely within an exemption, it must be disclosed. Exemptions must be narrowly construed.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 35 -->

## Exempt parts, not exempt records

An exemption usually covers some information in a record, not the whole thing. The office must black out the exempt part, give you the rest, and show how much it removed.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(f)(2): “Any reasonably segregable portion of a record shall be provided after deletion of the exempt information.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 15 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(f)(3): “The amount of information deleted shall be indicated on the released portion of the record”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 15 -->

## Working papers follow the office

The working-papers exemption covers five kinds of office and no others: the Governor, legislators, Supreme Court justices, Court of Appeals judges and the Attorney General.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(b)(7): “Unpublished memoranda, working papers, and correspondence of the Governor, members of the General Assembly, Supreme Court Justices, Court of Appeals Judges, and the Attorney General”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 9 -->

It does not reach state agencies, cities, counties or school districts, as the [Reporters Committee's Arkansas guide](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/arkansas/) notes. The exemption attaches to the office holding the record. If the Governor's office sent a message to a city, a county or a school district, the copy that office holds is its record, and the working-papers exemption does not cover it there. [Denied, now what](/manual/denied/) turns this into a step.

## Security services (Act 7 of 2023)

The September 2023 special session added an exemption for records about security for the Governor and other top state officials.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(b)(28): “Records that reflect the planning or provision of security services provided to the” Governor, the other constitutional officers, legislators and appellate judges.</p>
<!-- handbook p. 13 -->

[Act 7 of 2023](https://arkleg.state.ar.us/Acts/FTPDocument?path=%2FACTS%2F2023S1%2FPublic%2F&file=7.pdf&ddBienniumSession=2023%2F2023S1) did more than add the exemption. It created that exemption and made the Governor's part of it reach back to June 1, 2022. It amended a separate statute on Governor's Mansion security information, § 12-8-108(c), with the same reach-back date. It also requires the State Police to report the Governor's protection-detail expenses to the Legislative Council every quarter, by month and budget category.

<p class="law">Act 7 of 2023 (1st Ex. Sess.), § 4(b): “Arkansas Code § 25-19-105(b)(28)(A), created in Section 3 of this act, is retroactive to June 1, 2022.”</p>
<!-- not in the handbook: Act 7 of 2023 (1st Ex. Sess.) as passed, arkleg PDF p. 2, § 4(b) -->

The exemption covers security planning and services. Earlier drafts in the same session would have added a "deliberative process" exemption, exempted documents prepared by an attorney, and made it harder to recover legal fees; those were dropped before the final bill passed ([Arkansas Democrat-Gazette, Sept. 15, 2023](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2023/sep/15/sanders-signs-bill-to-modify-sunshine-law/)).

## Personnel and evaluation records

Personnel files are open except where release would be a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(b)(12): “Personnel records to the extent that disclosure would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 10 -->

Evaluation and job-performance records are harder to get. They open only after a suspension or termination has been finally resolved, when the records were a basis for it, and when there is a compelling public interest.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(c)(1): “shall be open to public inspection only upon final administrative resolution of any suspension or termination proceeding”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 13 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(c)(1): “and if there is a compelling public interest in their disclosure.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 13 -->

When a custodian decides a personnel or evaluation request, you, the custodian or the employee may ask the Attorney General for an opinion, which must come within three working days. The records stay closed until it does.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(c)(3)(B)(i): “seek an opinion from the Attorney General, who, within three (3) working days of receipt of the request, shall issue an opinion”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 14 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(c)(3)(B)(ii): “the custodian shall not disclose the records until the Attorney General has issued his or her opinion.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 14 -->

A request for nothing but gross salary skips that process: the employee is not notified first and cannot seek the opinion.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(c)(4): “If a request for public records seeks only the gross salary of a public employee or a set of public employees”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 14 -->

## Open investigations

Police files on investigations of suspected crimes are exempt while the investigations are undisclosed.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(b)(6): “Undisclosed investigations by law enforcement agencies of suspected criminal activity”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 9 -->

## Claims that are not exemptions

**Attorney-client privilege.** An office that sends a record to its lawyer does not make it secret. The Arkansas Supreme Court held in *Scott v. Smith* that neither the attorney-client privilege nor work product is an exemption under the act.

<p class="law">Scott v. Smith, 292 Ark. 174 (1987), as the handbook summarizes it: “there is no exception for the attorney-client privilege or work-product doctrine concerning state-agency records in the possession of its attorney”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 28 -->

**"Confidential" or "internal."** Neither word is an exemption by itself. A denial has to name the exemption it relies on; [Your rights](/manual/your-rights/#a-written-answer) quotes the duty.

**Mixed records.** An office may not refuse a whole record because part of it is exempt; the rule is above, and [Denied, now what](/manual/denied/) shows how to use it.

Other statutes outside the act also make some records confidential, and the act honors them. The handbook lists many of them on pages 51 to 53.
