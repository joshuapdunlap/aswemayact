Arkansas opens its records to its own citizens, and Arkansas agencies say so on their request pages. Knowing the rule, and the one workaround the Attorney General has recognized, saves a wasted request.

## The citizen rule

The right to inspect and copy belongs to "any citizen of the State of Arkansas," and the right to make a request is phrased the same way.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(1)(A): “all public records shall be open to inspection and copying … by any citizen of the State of Arkansas”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 8 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(2)(A): “A citizen may make a request to the custodian to inspect, copy … or receive copies of public records.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 8 -->

A business counts. The Arkansas Supreme Court has held that a corporation doing business in the state may use the act like any other requester.

<p class="law">Ark. Hwy. &amp; Transp. Dep’t v. Hope Brick Works, Inc., 294 Ark. 490 (1988), as the handbook summarizes it: “a corporation doing business in this state is a party entitled to information.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 28 -->

## Who is shut out

A person who has pleaded guilty to or been convicted of a felony and is in prison at the time of the request may not use the act. Neither may someone acting for that person, unless it is the person's lawyer asking for records the act would release anyway.

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(1)(B)(i): “has pleaded guilty to or been found guilty of a felony and is incarcerated in a correctional facility”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 8 -->

<p class="law">§ 25-19-105(a)(1)(B)(ii): “unless the representative is the person’s attorney who is requesting information that is subject to disclosure under this section.”</p>
<!-- handbook p. 8 -->

## Agencies may ask

An agency may ask you to show who you are or to fill in a form about citizenship, the Reporters Committee's guide says, citing Attorney General Opinion 94-235. The opinion makes the point only in a footnote, quoting a treatise: agencies "can presumably require documentation," since otherwise they could not check citizenship.

<p class="law">RCFP Open Government Guide, Arkansas: “An agency may require identification showing name and address or completion of a form on which the requester provides citizenship information.”</p>
<!-- not in the handbook: RCFP Open Government Guide, Arkansas, citing Op. Att’y Gen. 94-235 -->

Arkansas offices put the rule in writing. Little Rock's [City Attorney](https://littlerock.gov/government/city-departments/city-attorneys-office/freedom-of-information-act/) and [Police Department](https://littlerock.gov/residents/police-department/professional-standards/freedom-of-information-act-request) both list as a requirement that "the person making the request must be an Arkansas citizen." Bentonville's [request page](https://www.bentonvillear.com/1942/FOIA-Information) asks for a name and email to “[v]erify eligibility under Arkansas law.”

## If you live outside Arkansas

Two Attorney General opinions, 97-071 and 96-190, note in passing that someone outside Arkansas can have an Arkansas citizen make the request for them. The [Reporters Committee's guide](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/arkansas/) cites a third, 2008-191, but that opinion restates the citizen rule and does not address a citizen asking for someone else. The citizen is then the requester: the request goes out in that person's name, the answer comes back to that person, and any follow-up runs through that person too. Attorney General opinions are advice, not rulings, but agencies and courts read them.

## In practice

<ol class="step-list" role="list">
<li>

Say in the request that you are a citizen of Arkansas. One sentence is enough, and it heads off a reply asking.

</li>
<li>

If the office uses a form or portal with a citizenship question, answer it. If an office asks for identification, it may ask for your name and address, as the Reporters Committee guide notes above. Your reason for wanting the record generally does not matter; [Your rights](/manual/your-rights/) quotes the handbook on that. Personnel records are the exception: there the reason can count. In *Stilley v. McBride*, 332 Ark. 306 (1998), the Arkansas Supreme Court weighed a requester's only reason for wanting two police officers' home addresses, a cheaper way to serve them with a lawsuit, against their privacy, and the addresses stayed closed.

</li>
<li>

If you are not a citizen, find one who will file for you and who is willing to receive the records and follow up. Agree in advance how the records will reach you.

</li>
<li>

If you are asking on behalf of a business that operates in Arkansas, say so and name the business.

</li>
</ol>

Who you are does not change what the records cost or how fast they must come. Those rules are on [What it costs](/manual/what-it-costs/) and [The clock](/manual/the-clock/).
