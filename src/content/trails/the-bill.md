## Stop 1

The law this site explains is not fixed. The General Assembly can change it in any session, and some changes move fast. On Monday, March 27, 2023, a bill titled “To amend the Freedom of Information Act of 1967” was filed in the House as HB 1726. It would have added an exemption for an agency’s “deliberative process” records, let custodians charge for staff time past eight hours, and stretched the wait for a record in active use from three working days to ten. The legislature’s own page for the bill shows it on the agenda of the House State Agencies and Governmental Affairs Committee at 2:30 p.m. on Wednesday, March 29. That left two days between filing and hearing.

## Stop 2

It failed in that committee. Talk Business & Politics reported that private citizens came to speak against it, and the Democrat-Gazette reported more than three hours of testimony from opponents and supporters. The outlets give different vote counts, so I won’t repeat one; the bill page links the video of the hearing. The bill died when the session ended.

It wasn’t the only one that year. HB 1610 would have made a gathering of a local board a public meeting only when a quorum was present. The House committee voted it down on a voice vote in March, the [Arkansas Advocate reported](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2023/03/15/arkansas-house-committee-votes-down-bill-to-loosen-public-meetings-law/). An amended version passed the House, and the [bill’s history](https://arkleg.state.ar.us/Bills/Detail?id=HB1610&ddBienniumSession=2023%2F2023R) shows it died in the Senate committee.

## Stop 3

The House rules give the public a place in that room. Rule 61(a) says that everyone who wants to testify before a committee “shall be given a reasonable opportunity to do so as determined by a majority of the committee,” and that no oral or written statement is needed first. The committee decides how much time that is. House committee agendas go up at least eighteen hours ahead (Rule 60(a)), so the warning can be short. Each new House adopts its own rules, and I will check the 2027 ones when they are adopted. The House [told the public in 2025](https://www.arkansashouse.org/news/post/23021/2025-regular-session-to-begin-monday/) that people who want to speak for or against a bill can sign in to speak at the committee meeting.

## Stop 4

Bills that change the act usually carry the same title, “To amend the Freedom of Information Act of 1967,” and in each chamber they usually go to the State Agencies and Governmental Affairs Committee. That makes them findable. The legislature’s advanced search reads the text of bills; the link on this stop searches the 2027 session for that exact phrase. Today it finds nothing, because nothing has been filed. Once bills arrive it will also catch bills that only mention the act, so read the titles. A second search for a section number, such as 25-19-105 for records or 25-19-106 for meetings, catches some the first one misses.

Each bill’s page shows its text, every amendment, its committee and its status. It also lists every meeting where it was on the agenda, with a link to the video.

## Stop 5

Here is what I would do. Bills for the 2027 session can be filed starting November 16, 2026, and the session convenes January 11, 2027. Run the search on the last stop every week or so from mid-November. When a bill turns up, add it to the legislature’s free [Personalized Bill Tracking](https://arkleg.state.ar.us/Bills/TrackingLogin), which sends a daily email when a tracked bill changes.

Find your own representative and senator with the [District Finder](https://districtfinder.youraedi.com/), and check again after the November election, since new members are seated in January. Write to them about the bill before it is heard, and say what it would do to a request like yours. When the bill lands on a committee agenda, go and sign in to speak, or watch the video and write again before the floor vote. Read the bill itself, not only the title: a title like “to amend” can open the act up or close it down.
