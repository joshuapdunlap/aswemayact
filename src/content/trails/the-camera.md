## Stop 1

I start this trail in Fort Smith because its cameras are up for review and the reporting is recent. In October 2026 Talk Business & Politics asked Fort Smith’s seven city directors whether the city should keep its Flock license-plate cameras. One answered. The police department told the paper it has 44 cameras on Flock’s system, 21 of them plate readers, under a contract that runs through November 30, 2027. The department has cut how long it keeps plate data from thirty days to seven, stopped other agencies from using it, and is reviewing whether to renew. Any renewal, the report says, will go before the board in public.

## Stop 2

A camera program produces records you can ask for. When Greenwood’s council took up its cameras in August 2026, the city attorney’s memo supporting them pointed to short data retention, audit logs, restricted sharing and a human check before any stop. Each of those claims rests on a record: the contract and its renewals, the department’s written policy, the retention setting, the list of agencies the data is shared with, and the audit log of searches. Ask for them, and you can check each claim against its record.

## Stop 3

DeFlock, a volunteer project, maps license-plate cameras from OpenStreetMap entries. Search for Fort Smith on its map to see where the mapped cameras stand and which way they face. Volunteers add the cameras, so one that isn’t on the map may still exist. The map also can’t tell you who runs a camera or how long it keeps data. Those answers are in the records from the last stop. The [source cards](/manual/sources/) list DeFlock with its limits.

## Stop 4

Since late July 2026, Centerton, Greers Ferry, Searcy, Mayflower, Farmington and Pea Ridge have ended their Flock contracts or let them lapse, and Cabot unplugged its cameras while it decides, the Arkansas Advocate reported. The article puts the moves down to privacy concerns and to cost and administration. It also reported that Little Rock’s cameras read the plates of 687,332 unique vehicles in thirty days.

## Stop 5

Here is what I would ask for. If your city runs plate readers, [write a request](/manual/write/) for the contract and any renewal, the department’s policy on the cameras, the retention setting, the list of agencies with access to the data, and the audit log of searches for a recent month. Name each document. A request for “communications about the cameras” can come back without the contract in it.

When the renewal or the policy comes before your council or board at a regular meeting, a body with a website or social media page has to post the agenda online at least three days ahead. Bring what you got and speak during public comment. In Fort Smith that decision belongs to the Board of Directors, and that is where I would take the records.
