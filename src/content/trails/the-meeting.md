## Stop 1

Since August 5, 2025, an Arkansas public body that has a website or a social media page has to post its meetings there. That means the time, place and date, and the most current agenda, at least three days before a regular meeting and at least two hours before a special or emergency one. Nothing gathers these postings in one place, so you go to each body’s own site. The manual’s page on [the clock](/manual/the-clock/) sets out the deadlines.

## Stop 2

Here is one. The City of Little Rock posts its Board of Directors agendas on the city’s website. The agenda for the October 5, 2026 meeting lists the resolutions and ordinances the board planned to take up, each with a line on what it does. Item M-3 is an ordinance to issue limited tax general obligation capital improvement bonds.

Bond votes run on a clock of their own. Under Act 992 of 2025, a court can undo a vote authorizing debt over an open-meetings violation only if the challenge comes within thirty days ([the clock](/manual/the-clock/) has the details). An agenda tells you what will be decided and when. It doesn’t tell you what was said.

## Stop 3

A meeting leaves records behind: the agenda, any packet handed to members, the minutes, and a recording. Once those exist, they are public records, and you can ask for them like any other. Ask for each by name, and ask for the recording as the original audio or video file.

Recordings have decided cases. In 2024 the West Memphis School District settled a suit over special meetings it held without notice and did not record, agreeing to pay the plaintiffs’ fees and a $1,500 fine, [the Democrat-Gazette reported](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/arkansas-democrat-gazette/20240227/281822878740580).

## Stop 4

In April 2026 two men sued the Pine Bluff School District and its board in circuit court. They allege 13 violations between July 2025 and February 2026: meetings with no recording at all, recordings without audible sound, public comment left off the recording, and recording that didn’t resume after an executive session. The suit cites § 25-19-106(d)(1) as requiring open meetings to be recorded so that the sound is captured. One of the plaintiffs said he had warned the board about it during public comment and been brushed off. These are allegations. The district’s attorney declined to comment, and I have found no reported ruling.

## Stop 5

Pick a body that governs something you care about, whether a city board, a quorum court, a school board or a water district. Find its posted agenda. Then [write a request](/manual/write/) for the minutes and the recording of its last meeting, and ask for the original file. Compare what comes back with the agenda.

Then go to the next meeting. Many bodies take public comment, and the agenda usually says how to sign up. Say what you found, and point to the record.
