# How to write a records request that can't be dodged

A request is a short letter, and every sentence in it can matter later. In recent Arkansas
cases, requesters lost because of what their letter did or did not say.

**Put these eight things in writing.**

1. **To whom, and who you are.** Address the custodian, and say you are an Arkansas citizen.
2. **Be specific enough to find.** Give dates, names, offices and subjects, and file or
   contract numbers if you know them. The law asks for enough detail for the custodian to
   find the records "with reasonable effort" (§ 25-19-105(a)(2)(C)).
3. **Name the kinds of documents.** List them: contracts, agreements, invoices, emails, text
   messages, calendars, recordings. In one 2026 case a judge ruled against a resident who
   asked for communications but did not name the agreement itself. Asking for
   "communications" may not reach a contract.
4. **Ask for the format.** Ask for electronic copies in the format the office already has:
   spreadsheets as spreadsheets, recordings as files (§ 25-19-105(d)(2)(B)). The office does
   not have to create new records or answer questions, so ask for records.
5. **For recordings, ask for the original files**, and for a list of the devices that made
   them.
6. **Number each item, and ask for a plain answer to each**: the records, or a statement
   that none exist, or the exemption that applies (§ 25-19-105(a)(3)).
7. **Ask for an itemized estimate** before any copies are made.
8. **Keep a dated copy.** Send by email when you can, and keep the sent message. Deadlines
   run from the date you asked.

**If part of a record is exempt,** ask for the exempt part to be blacked out and the rest
released, with each redaction marked.

**Before you send it,** read it as the custodian will. Could an honest clerk find every item?
Could a reluctant one answer your letter and still leave out the record you most want? If
so, name that record by type, date and the people involved.
