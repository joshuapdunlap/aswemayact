# Your rights to public records in Arkansas

**The Arkansas Freedom of Information Act** (Ark. Code Ann. § 25-19-101 and after) lets any
Arkansas citizen see and copy the records that state and local governments keep. You do
not need to be a reporter or a lawyer, and you do not need to say why you want them.

**Records in any form.** Paper, email, text messages, databases, photographs, audio and
video are all covered (§ 25-19-103(15)(A)). Records kept by a public office, or by a public
employee doing the job, are presumed public unless a specific law makes them exempt.

**Who has to answer.** State agencies, cities, counties, school districts and other bodies
that run on public money.

**How to ask.**

1. Find the **custodian**, the person in charge of the record: usually a clerk, a records
   officer or a department's FOIA contact.
2. Ask in person, by phone, by mail, by email or through the office's online form
   (§ 25-19-105(a)(2)(B)). **A written request is best**: it leaves a dated record, and it
   triggers the written answer described below.
3. You may look at records for free during office hours, and copy them yourself with a
   phone camera (§ 25-19-105(a)(1)(A)).
4. Records are due **when you ask**. A delay is allowed only if the record is in active use
   or in storage, and the custodian must then say so in writing and give you a date and hour
   no more than three working days away (§ 25-19-105(e)).
5. Copies may cost only what copying actually costs (§ 25-19-105(d)(3)). For an ordinary
   request, staff time is not part of the bill; police recordings and some electronic work
   have their own rules.

**What the written answer must say** (§ 25-19-105(a)(3)):

- if no records exist, that **no records exist**;
- if records are withheld, **which exemption** applies; "confidential" alone is not enough;
- if the office does not hold the records, **who does**, when it knows.

A custodian who knowingly fails to give that written answer can face a criminal penalty
(§ 25-19-105(a)(5)).

**What the law does not do.** It does not open every record, and Arkansas has no office that
enforces it for you. If you are refused, see *Denied, now what* on the site.
